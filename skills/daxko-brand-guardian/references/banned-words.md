## Banned Constructions

- Passive voice — always use active voice
- Hedging: "might," "could potentially," "may want to consider"
- Generic CTAs: "learn more," "click here," "contact us," "submit"
- Filler weakeners: "just," "very," "really," "basically," "actually," "literally"
- Stacked exclamation marks (one is acceptable sparingly; three is excessive; never stack)
- ALL CAPS for emphasis (use bold or sentence structure instead)
- Excessive em dashes and hyphens (Boutique especially — point to metrics and customer stories instead)

## Nonprofit-Specific Bans

> Source: NP-02-Brand-Standards. Absolute bans for all NP market output.

**Banned words (NP market):**
- impact
- accelerate
- gym
- "[x], together" (any "X, together" construction)

**Banned framing (NP market):**
- Aggressive urgency: "limited time," "act now," "don't miss out" (kills trust with nonprofit buyers)
- Commercial sales language in prospect-facing content: "revenue," "close," "sell," "churn rate," "CLV," "conversion rate"
- AI-as-headcount-reduction framing — never imply AI reduces staffing or funding. Frame as freeing staff to focus on mission
- Technical AI language with NP audiences: "LLMs," "machine learning," "neural networks" — lead with outcomes, not technology
- AI as bolt-on or add-on product — always frame as embedded in the platform they already pay for
- Experimental or roadmap AI language — NP buyers need confidence and stability; anchor to delivered capabilities only

## Boutique-Specific Bans

> Source: BO-05-Agent-Config. Style and tone guidance for Zen Planner and Exercise.com voice.

**Banned constructions (Boutique market):**
- Excessive em dashes and hyphens — boutique owners detect corporate-speak quickly
- Generic marketing language without metrics — always point to specific numbers and real customer stories
- Vendor-style positioning — speak peer-to-peer with owners, not as a corporate vendor

**Required framing (Boutique market):**
- Always highlight "taking admin off your plate" or equivalent owner-time-back angle
- Always reference specific proof points (e.g., 62M workouts logged on SugarWOD; CrossFit 1926 +$10,690 revenue with Flex Fees)

## Club-Specific Bans

> Source: CL-02-Brand-Standards. Lead with ROI and outcomes, not mission language.

**Banned framing (Club market):**
- Mission-driven or community-impact language — that belongs to the Nonprofit market
- Generic operational claims without metrics — always anchor to revenue per member, card approval rates, retention rates, or staff productivity
- Corporate tone — keep messaging direct and peer-to-peer with club operators

## Market-Specific Quick Reference

| Market | Voice Anchor | Lead With | Avoid |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nonprofit | Practical and experienced — confident but measured | Mission outcomes, community results, board-trusted reporting | "Impact," "accelerate," "gym," "[x], together," urgency tactics, technical AI language |
| Club | Bold and confident — peer-to-peer with operators | ROI, revenue per member, card approval rates, retention | Mission language, generic claims without metrics, corporate tone |
| Boutique | Community-focused, warm, energetic — owner-to-owner | Owner time back, specific customer stories, transparent pricing | Excessive em dashes, vendor positioning, generic marketing speak |
