# ✅ GOOD — a boutique source repurposed into a traced set

**This is the standard.** Read it before you write. The template gives you the shape; this gives you the bar:
how a source is mapped before anything is written, how a wrong source claim is stopped instead of multiplied, how
every piece shows where it came from, and how honest the trace table is.

> **Built only from facts in the bundled files.** Every figure, product description and rule below is in
> `references/`. The source is a realistic Daxko-style blog post written for this example; its four deliberate
> errors are the point of the lesson. **Never carry a fact from this file into a real set** — re-read the bundled
> file instead.

**The request that produced it**

> "Here's our blog post from last month. Turn it into two LinkedIn posts, a three-email series, a 60-second video
> script and some pull quotes."
>

>

>

> membership dropped 4%.
>
> ¶5 The owner of CrossFit 1926 told us: "[OWNER QUOTE — in a real source the owner's sentence sits here; this
> example does not invent customer words]"
>
> ¶6 Want to see what processing costs are taking out of your margin? Book a walkthrough.

**Why this is a good set, in one line each:**

- The source was mapped and checked **before** any piece was written, so the two wrong claims in ¶4 never reached
  a single piece.
- The one clash no fixed rule decides (what Flex Fees does) was **asked first**, in plain words, with a default;
  the clashes a fixed rule decides (the itemised figure, the banned word, the quote) were applied without asking.
- Every piece carries its trace, so a reviewer can check any line against the source in seconds.
- The source's own hedge — *"can help protect"* — survives in every piece that uses ¶3. Nothing got stronger by
  getting shorter.
- The owner quote is used verbatim and **bracketed in every piece that carries it**, because nobody here can verify
  written approval.
- The trace table admits a framing rule the set cannot meet, rather than inventing a time saving to meet it.
- One offer, the last line.

## First reply — the ask-first question (the whole reply)

Step 4 found four clashes. Three are decided by fixed rules: the itemised figure beats the summarised one, the
banned word takes its approved replacement, and the unapproved customer quote is used verbatim and bracketed. One
is not: the source and our files disagree about what Flex Fees does. So the first reply is only this:

```
Before I write the set, one question:

1. Your piece says Flex Fees adds revenue by catching failed payments; our files say Flex Fees does not recover
   failed payments — it passes card and ACH transaction costs to the member. Which should the new pieces use?
   1 — what our files say: Flex Fees passes the processing cost to the member at the point of payment
   2 — what your piece says: Flex Fees catches failed payments
   3 — leave the mechanism out of every piece
   Reply 1 or 2, or say **go** to use 1.

Your answer makes this stronger, and the answer will change with your choice.
```

No file name, no line number, no piece, no offer. **The person replied:** *"go"*. The next reply is the set.

## Seven things this example is teaching

1. **Check before you write.** The SOURCE CHECK table exists before the first piece. A clash found after writing
   gets patched into some pieces and missed in others.
2. **The source is the fact base, not an approval.** ¶4 is in a published post and is still wrong. Seven pieces
   restating it would be seven wrong lines.
3. **An itemised figure beats a summarised one.** The bundled file breaks the $10,690.08 down; the source's
   $10,690 credited to Flex Fees overstates the product more than four times.
4. **The hedge survives compression.** *"Can help protect"* is in every piece that uses ¶3, and *"every box owner
   we talk to"* never shrinks to *"every box owner"*.
5. **A quote nobody can verify is bracketed everywhere it travels**, not only in the gaps table: the bracket is what
   reaches whoever pastes the line.
6. **When a rule cannot be met without inventing, say so.** The time-back framing is declared unmet, and the reply
   says plainly that our files don't name who can grant an exception — it never guesses a name.
7. **Ask first when your piece and our files disagree and no fixed rule decides it.** One plain question, numbered
   options, a default, no file names — then the set, with the pick marked "your choice". A clash a fixed rule
   decides is never asked.
