# ❌ BAD — do not produce anything like this

> 🔴 **WARNING. Every piece of copy in section A of this file is WRONG and must never be copied, adapted or
> reused.** It is reproduced so the failure is recognisable, because this failure reads *better* than the good
> one. It is fluent, confident, on-message, and it multiplies every error in its source.
>
> **This is the repurposer's double failure.** *Invention*: pieces that say more than the source, because
> shortening invites a stronger claim. *Laundering*: pieces that faithfully copy what the source said when the
> source was wrong, so one bad line becomes seven.

**The request and source that produced it** — the same as `good-repurposed-set.md`: the blog post *The fee
nobody budgets for*, six paragraphs, with *"Flex Fees added $10,690 to their revenue by catching failed
payments"* in ¶4, the banned word *"seamless"* in ¶2, a hedged claim in ¶3 and an owner quote in ¶5. Asked for:
two LinkedIn posts, a three-email series, a 60-second video script and some pull quotes.

## Why this is the more dangerous of the two files

Read section A again without the annotations. It sounds like a confident marketer. Every figure looks specific,
the quote sounds human, and the source **really did say** two of the worst lines. That is laundering: a reviewer
who checks the pieces against the source finds them faithful, and the error ships seven times. **The only defence
is checking the source against the bundled files before writing anything**, and showing that check.
