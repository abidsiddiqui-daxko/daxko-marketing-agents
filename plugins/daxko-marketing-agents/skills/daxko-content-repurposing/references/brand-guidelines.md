## Brand Voice

**Overall:** Engaging, credible, and aligned with our mission. Focus on addressing customer challenges, fostering trust, and reinforcing commitment to customer success.

### Voice rules by market

**Nonprofit:**
- Lead with community outcomes, not product features
- Mission-first tone; earn credibility before selling
- Use nonprofit-native language: members, programs, registrations, mission, community
- Push toward proof-led, specific claims (avoid fluffy marketing)
- Example of correct register: "20 million members. Billions of transactions. This is what Daxko is building AI on."

**Nonprofit segment voice distinctions:**
- YMCA / JCC: operational clarity + board trust
- BGC: multi-site complexity + movement partnership
- Community Rec: efficiency + simplicity

**Club:**
- Lead with ROI and measurable outcomes (revenue, retention, payment recovery)
- Metrics-first language
- Avoid mission-driven or community-centric framing (that belongs to Nonprofit)
- Tone: direct, peer-to-peer with operators, not corporate

**Boutique:**
- Speak to the passion and purpose behind the business
- Respect that owners wear many hats — emphasize simplicity
- Use social proof from similar businesses
- Highlight quick wins and immediate value
- Be authentic — boutique owners detect corporate speak
- **Refrain from excessive em dashes or hyphens**
- Point to metrics and real customer stories (boutique owners want proof)
- Highlight "taking admin off your plate"

## AI Messaging Guardrails

### Core narrative

Daxko is embedding AI directly into the operational systems customers already trust. This is not experimentation — it is reducing manual work, improving consistency, and strengthening engagement outcomes. AI at Daxko is part of how the platform operates, not a separate tool teams must learn, manage, or secure on their own.

### Always emphasize
- Trust, Security, and Choice
- Operational Relief
- Mission Alignment (NP) / Revenue and retention outcomes (Club) / Growth and admin relief (Boutique)

### Never say
- Aggressive replacement narratives
- "One AI tool to rule them all"
- Anything that minimizes or dismisses partner value
- Overly technical AI language (LLMs, neural networks, etc.) — lead with outcomes
- That AI is experimental or unproven — always anchor to delivered capabilities

### Market-specific AI guardrails

**Nonprofit:**
- NEVER imply AI reduces headcount, staffing, or funding
- NEVER suggest member data could be shared, aggregated across orgs, or used beyond the customer's environment
- NEVER position AI as a bolt-on or add-on
- Always tie AI outcomes to mission language: community impact, member retention, operational clarity, board-trusted reporting

**Club:**
- Always tie AI back to measurable outcomes: lead conversion, payment recovery, member retention
- Position it as embedded within Daxko Club Automation and Daxko Engage Pro — not as a separate tool
- Focus on how it improves daily operations for staff

**Boutique:**
- Frame AI as time-saving / admin-relieving
- Never make it sound enterprise-level or complex
- Position via Zen Planner Engage AI (Voice, Conversation, Workflow, Content, Reviews AI)

### Pre-approved objection responses

| Objection | Response |
|-----------|----------|
| "Is this replacing staff?" | "No. It's reducing manual work so staff can focus on mission-critical engagement / high-value tasks." |
| "Is our data safe?" | "Yes. AI operates inside the same secure environment that already protects member identity, payments, and permissions. Data is not used to train public models." |
| "We already use AI partners." | "That's great — Daxko is designed to support that. Over time, native capabilities may reduce overlap, but compatibility and choice remain." |

### AI voice guardrail
> If AI sounds like a feature to sell instead of work getting done, the message is off-voice.

## Tone by Content Type

| Format | Tone | Example |
|--------|------|---------|
| Blog posts | Conversational and educational — simplify complex ideas, direct and engaging | "Fewer steps. Faster workflows. More time on the mat." |
| Email campaigns | Direct, helpful, and action-oriented | "Automated billing means no more chasing payments." |
| Case studies / testimonials | Data-driven and story-led. Credit customer staff and leadership for outcomes — Daxko is the enabler, not the hero | "Recover an average of 69% of payments with Daxko's 30 years of experience in revenue recovery." |
| Social media | Energetic and community-focused | "Turn your vision into reality — starting today." |
| Sales enablement | Consultative and ROI-focused | "We've helped thousands of businesses streamline operations — yours can be next." |
| Competitive content | Factual and defensible — compare on merits, never attack competitors directly. Avoid vague superlatives | — |
| AI-related content | Practical and grounded. Preferred: "AI helps the system complete work that already exists." Avoid: "AI-powered everything", speculative AI language, or framing AI as a feature to sell | — |

## Words We Use / Words We Avoid

| Use | Avoid | Why |
|-----|-------|-----|
| Partner | Vendor | We are a partner, not a transactional vendor |
| Members | Users | Nonprofit and boutique language — people are members, not users. "Students" for martial arts, CrossFit, and youth programs |
| Empower / Streamline | Disrupt | Mission-aligned and outcome-focused language |
| Operational-reality language (Nonprofit) | Revolutionary / Cutting-edge / Game-changer | Per 2026 Brand Voice: no revolutionary language, no competitive posturing, no future-feature promises |
| "We" language | "I" (AI agent voice) | All agent outputs must use "we" language — never "I" |
| Multi-location environments, seasonal programs, cross-department workflows | Venture-backed SaaS language | Grounds messages in how nonprofits actually operate |

## Content Guardrails — Always / Never

### Always
- Lead with the customer's problem, not Daxko's product
- Include one clear CTA per piece
- Reference community impact for nonprofit content
- Use real metrics with source attribution for all claims
- Credit customer staff and leadership for outcomes, not Daxko
- Describe current capabilities accurately — never future features

### Never
- Make unsubstantiated ROI claims
- Use aggressive urgency tactics ("Last chance!" / "Act now!")
- Reference competitor names negatively — compare on merits only
- Publish customer stories without written approval
- Position Daxko as the hero of customer outcomes
- Use venture-backed SaaS language or hype
- Frame AI as replacing people or as a feature to sell
- Produce customer quotes from templates — only use approved pull quotes

### AI Content Guardrails
- **Daxko Nonprofit guardrail (verbatim):** "If a sentence would sound at home in a venture-backed SaaS pitch, it does not belong in Daxko messaging."
- **AI voice guardrail (verbatim):** "If AI sounds like a feature to sell instead of work getting done, the message is off-voice."
