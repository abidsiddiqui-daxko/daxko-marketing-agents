## 3. Target Markets and Personas

### Nonprofit Market

**Segments served:** YMCA, JCC, Boys and Girls Clubs, Community Recreation Centers

#### YMCA ICP

- **Scale:** 2,597 YMCA locations in the US, ~750 buying units (local associations). Daxko serves 637 as of Aug 2025.
- **Primary ICP:** Mid-sized to large YMCA associations — multi-branch or single large facility (5,000+ members)
- **Account Tiers:**
  - Tier 1 (Large Metro): multi-branch, 5+ branches, enterprise functionality
  - Tier 2 (Mid-sized Regional): 1-3 branches, several thousand members
  - Tier 3 (Small Community): single facility, budget-sensitive
- **Core personas:**
  - CEO / Executive Director (primary) — strategic vision, board accountability
  - COO / VP of Operations (primary) — operational efficiency, multi-branch coordination
  - CFO / Finance Director (secondary) — financial controls, grant compliance
  - IT Director / CIO (secondary) — integrations, data governance
  - Board Members (influencer) — mission alignment, fiduciary oversight
- **Buying dynamics:** Multi-layered, consensus-driven. Major investments require CEO sponsorship and board approval. Peer influence is significant — Ys consult Y-USA, regional networks, and neighboring Ys.
- **Pain points:** Managing multiple branches with inconsistent systems, complex program scheduling across departments, multiple revenue streams (memberships, fees, donations), post-pandemic recovery and growth
- **Qualification signals:** Managing multiple branches OR single large facility with 5,000+ members; wide range of programs (aquatics, fitness, childcare, camps); multiple revenue streams; experiencing growth, consolidation, or post-pandemic recovery
- **Enrichment signals:** New CEO/COO/CIO appointments; facility expansions, mergers, or capital campaigns; strategic plans referencing digital transformation; RFPs or job postings mentioning system integrations; prospects mentioning Salesforce or TractionRec evaluations

#### JCC ICP

- **Scale:** ~150-200+ JCCs nationally via JCC Association of North America. Daxko serves 51 as of Aug 2025.
- **Primary ICP:** Mid-sized to large JCCs with diverse programming — fitness, preschool, camps, cultural arts, senior services
- **Core personas:**
  - Executive Director (primary) — overall center leadership
  - Operations Director (primary) — day-to-day management
  - Finance Director (secondary) — budgets and compliance
  - Board Members (secondary) — capital investment decisions
- **Buying dynamics:** Decision-making centered on Executive Director with Finance Director and Board. Many JCCs rely on peer guidance and external consultants due to limited IT resources.
- **Pain points:** Complex membership reporting, disconnected camp data, training and adoption challenges, accumulated point solutions creating administrative friction
- **Market context:** No single technology provider has successfully built a solution tailored specifically for JCCs — this is a market opportunity. JCCs often perceive Daxko as outdated. Key competitors: TractionRec, Amilia (elevated threat — active FUD counter-campaign at daxko.com/considering-amilia), Xplor.
- **Migration proof points:** Rochester JCC, Siegel JCC (Amilia migrations)
- **Qualification signals:** Active fitness center and structured membership model; multiple overlapping programs; growing or modernizing facilities; increasing focus on donor engagement; accumulated point solutions creating friction; JCCs currently evaluating Amilia (priority displacement opportunity)

#### Boys and Girls Clubs ICP

- **Scale:** 4,000-5,500+ club sites, **~850 independent organizations** (corrected from prior 1,100 figure — confirmed with Constance). Daxko serves 31 as of Aug 2025. **2026 target: 27% market share = 229 customers.**
- **Primary ICP:** Mid-to-large BGC organizations managing multiple sites and youth participant volume — especially those under pressure to report outcomes to funders
- **Core personas:**
  - Executive Director / CEO (primary)
  - Board of Directors (secondary)
  - Operations / Club Services Director (secondary)
  - Development / Fundraising Director (secondary)
- **Buying dynamics:** Centralized and lean — Executive Director is key authority. Board scrutinizes ROI and mission alignment. BGCA national office influences data collection standards indirectly.
- **Pain points:** Managing hundreds to thousands of youth annually across multiple sites; year-round programs; attendance, enrollment, and outcomes reporting for funders; active fundraising through grants and corporate partnerships
- **Campaign theme (2026):** "Ready Now for Club Growth" — movement-centered, not migration-centered. Lead with readiness and scalability, not competitive displacement.
- **Differentiator update:** Remove "only purpose-built for BGCs" — MyClubHub now serves the BGC market. Correct position: Daxko is ready now and can scale with BGCs across single-site and multi-club organizations.
- **Competitors to flag:** Vision (transition narrative), MyClubHub (direct competitor, transition resource hub available)

#### Community Centers ICP

- **Scale:** 3,490 independent community recreation centers in US — $2B+ annual revenue, 52,882 employees
- **Primary ICP:** Multi-program nonprofit or municipal community centers managing registration, scheduling, payments, and program reporting
- **2026 new logo target:** ~$1.03M pipeline (Community Rec segment, from NL by NP Market Sgmt 2526 tab)
- **Core personas:**
  - Executive Director / Center Director (nonprofit centers)
  - Parks and Recreation Director (municipal centers)
- **Buying dynamics:** Nonprofit centers: ED + Board. Municipal centers: Parks and Rec Director + City IT/Procurement. Usability for non-technical staff and demonstrable community value are key drivers.
- **Competitors in this segment:** Tier 1 small: RecDesk, MyRec.com, EZFacility. Tier 2 mid-sized: ACTIVE Net, CivicRec, Amilia. Tier 3 large: RecTrac (strongest), Xplor Recreation, ACTIVE Net.
- **Top proof point:** Edgar May Community Center — +41% registrations, +314% donations, +11% memberships after switching to Daxko

### Club Market

**Segments served:** Fitness, Multi-Purpose, Campus Recreation, Corporate Wellness, Country Club, HOA, Hotel, Medical Fitness and Recovery, Eatertainment (emerging), Racquet

#### Single-Location Health Club / Gym

- **Profile:** 500-5,000 members, for-profit, community gym or boutique health club
- **Primary buyer:** Club Owner / Operator — makes all technology decisions, focused on ROI and revenue growth
- **Secondary personas:** General Manager, Membership Sales Director, Finance / Billing Manager
- **Pain points:** Manual billing and payment failures draining staff time; member churn with no early warning signals; scattered tech stack; difficulty converting leads to members (no automated follow-up)
- **Sales cycle:** 3-6 months, ROI-driven
- **Key metrics they care about:** Revenue per member, member acquisition cost, card decline rate, churn/retention rate, staff productivity

#### Multi-Location Fitness Operator

- **Profile:** 2-50+ locations, regional or national footprint, 5,000-50,000+ members, franchise or corporate-owned
- **Primary buyer:** VP of Operations / COO — responsible for standardizing operations across locations
- **Secondary personas:** CFO / Finance Director, IT Director, Membership Sales Director
- **Pain points:** Inconsistent member experience across locations; no consolidated reporting across sites; complex corporate membership management; high implementation and migration risk
- **Sales cycle:** 6-12 months, committee-based, requires C-suite sign-off
- **Decision drivers:** References from comparable multi-site operators; implementation timeline and risk management

#### Medical Fitness and Specialty Wellness

- **Profile:** Hospital-based wellness centers, cardiac rehab facilities, and adaptive fitness organizations
- **Primary buyer:** Director of Wellness / Director of Fitness
- **Pain points:** Managing complex membership categories (patients, staff, community); integration with healthcare systems; outcome reporting for accreditation and funders; adaptive equipment and program scheduling

#### Club Buying-Cycle Signals
- Announcing a new location opening or acquisition
- Job postings for Membership Director, Billing Manager, or IT Manager

- Attending trade shows — IHRSA, Club Industry, or regional fitness expos
- Franchise network issuing RFP for standardized software
- Current system is Jonas, ABC Fitness, or Mindbody — known migration triggers

## 4. Competitive Landscape

### Nonprofit Competitors

#### TractionRec
- **What they offer:** CRM-based solution built on Salesforce for community and nonprofit organizations. Uses Salesforce foundations plus managed packages for membership, program registration, childcare, and fundraising.
- **HQ:** Canada
- **Target:** YMCAs, JCCs, Parks and Rec, BGCAs via the Salesforce ecosystem. Founded 2015 and growing.
- **Strengths:** Salesforce-native platform (flexibility, customization, enterprise integrations); AI/Agentforce positioning — 24/7 registration agents, guided enrollment, always-on member support; modern AI-forward messaging
- **Weaknesses:** Requires ongoing Salesforce admin governance — permissions, workflow maintenance, release testing become a hidden operating cost. Steeper learning curve for frontline staff. Salesforce licenses + add-ons expand TCO. Risk of over-engineering simple processes. Platform dependency on Salesforce release cycles.
- **Trap question:** "Who on your team will own Salesforce admin, testing, and ongoing AI tuning — and what happens to your operations when that person leaves?"
- **Win themes:** Purpose-built nonprofit operations vs. CRM-configured governance burden. Daxko = vendor-managed simplicity; daily workflows do not require Salesforce specialists. Predictable value. AI outcomes grounded in operational data. Y-USA alliance.

#### Amilia
- **What they offer:** eCommerce platform (Amilia SmartRec) for recreation organizations: program/class registration, facility booking, membership management, invoicing, and CRM. Founded 2009, Montreal.
- **HQ:** Montreal, Canada
- **Target:** Recreation departments, municipalities, sports organizations, and small to mid-sized community organizations. Active growth in JCC and community rec segments.
- **Strengths:** User-friendly interface for small-to-mid orgs; strong eCommerce and online registration; cost-effective subscription; fast implementation; positioning AI as "responsible recreation technology" thought leader
- **Weaknesses:** No dedicated fundraising module (workaround required); limited membership depth for large nonprofits; no built-in risk management tools; dated UI; users report duplicate accounts, billing errors, and inaccurate reporting; sales team overpromises features that do not materialize; not a true all-in-one for larger orgs
- **Trap question:** "How do you handle donor management and grant compliance today — and when your finance team needs to reconcile transactions before a board meeting, how many systems do they touch?"
- **Counter-campaign:** Active FUD landing page at daxko.com/considering-amilia
- **Migration proof points:** Rochester JCC, Siegel JCC
- **Win themes:** Real-time accurate data vs. reporting gaps. Built-in fundraising vs. no native module. Risk management built in vs. none. Scalability for multi-site vs. best suited for smaller orgs. AI operationalized inside trusted workflows vs. Amilia's AI thought leadership without delivery depth.

#### ACTIVENet (ACTIVE Network)
- **What they offer:** Comprehensive event, activity, and program management platform for parks and rec departments and community organizations.
- **Weaknesses:** Complex pricing structure and high transaction fees; fragmented data across modules; not purpose-built for nonprofit membership models
- **Win themes:** Purpose-built nonprofit operations with integrated fundraising and donor management; transparent, lower total cost of ownership

#### Personify
- **HQ:** Austin, TX
- **Target:** Large nonprofits, associations
- **Pricing model:** Enterprise licensing
- **Strengths:** Feature depth, enterprise scale
- **Weaknesses:** Complexity, slow implementation, enterprise-heavy and not designed for daily staff workflows
- **Trap question:** "How long did your Personify implementation take, and how many staff hours per month does your team spend on system administration and customization maintenance?"
- **Win themes:** Simpler implementation and faster time to value; purpose-built for fitness/wellness member management; lower total cost of ownership for mid-sized nonprofits; better frontline UX

#### RecTrac (Vermont Systems)
- **HQ:** Vermont
- **Target:** Parks, rec, nonprofits — particularly large community rec and municipal centers
- **Pricing model:** Traditional licensing
- **Strengths:** Feature depth, stability, strong in large community rec segment
- **Weaknesses:** Legacy technology, dated UI, client-server deployment, no built-in CRM or fundraising
- **Trap question:** "When your membership team wants to run a re-engagement campaign for lapsed members, what does that workflow look like today — and how many systems are involved?"
- **Win themes:** Modern SaaS vs. legacy client-server; built-in fundraising vs. none; Daxko Engage CRM and marketing automation vs. no built-in CRM

#### Neon One (NEON CRM)
- **HQ:** Chicago, IL
- **Target:** Small-mid nonprofits
- **Pricing model:** SaaS subscription
- **Strengths:** Fundraising focus, integrations, connected fundraising ecosystem
- **Weaknesses:** Not membership-centric, lacks operations depth for YMCAs/JCCs/BGCs
- **Trap question:** "When a member registers for a program and then makes a donation, are those two records connected in your system today — or do your membership and fundraising teams work from separate databases?"
- **Win themes:** Daxko is a full operations platform — Neon One is CRM/fundraising only with no membership or program management depth. One system for operations, payments, engagement, and reporting vs. requiring integration with a separate ops platform. Purpose-built for YMCAs/JCCs/BGCs vs. generic small-mid nonprofits.

#### MyClubHub (BGC segment)
- **Target:** Boys & Girls Clubs — direct competitor in BGC segment as of 2026
- **Strategic context:** Previous Daxko differentiator of "only purpose-built for BGCs" is no longer valid. MyClubHub transition resource hub is part of required deliverables. BGC campaign is movement-centered ("Ready Now for Club Growth"), not migration-centered.
- **Win themes:** Readiness and scalability across single-site and multi-club organizations vs. uniqueness claim

- **SGA:** Well-suited for sports and camp; strong financial management. Not a full nonprofit operational management solution.

### Club Competitors

#### ABC Fitness Solutions
- **What they offer:** Full-service fitness software for large franchises and multi-location facilities. Strong in payment processing (full-service payfac, next-day ACH funding), financial reporting, and franchise management with member reciprocity.
- **Target:** Large franchise operators and national chains (e.g., Planet Fitness) needing centralized payment routing and member reciprocity.
- **Strengths:** Full-service payfac with next-day ACH; strong franchise and multi-location management; high brand recognition among large chains; ability to pass transaction fees
- **Weaknesses:** Rigid system — up to 7-10 days delay for basic changes; slow customer support; limited dynamic reporting; no accrual accounting support; restrictive and expensive API access; mobile app lacks native core system integration (delayed sync)
- **Trap question:** "How much control do you actually have today over your billing, refunds, and when your funds hit your account?"
- **Win themes:** Self-service flexibility and speed of change; responsive dedicated support; real-time flexible reporting with accrual accounting; open API at competitive rates; unified mobile with real-time sync

#### Mindbody (Club Segment)
- **What they offer:** Broad wellness platform for gyms, studios, salons, and spas — not purpose-built for club operators.
- **Weaknesses:** Built for salons and spas, not purpose-built for gyms; operators report feeling like "just a number"; impersonal and slow support; no native access control; 20% marketplace fee per new client
- **Trap question:** "How are you currently managing family memberships or non-class offerings like courts, childcare, or programs?"
