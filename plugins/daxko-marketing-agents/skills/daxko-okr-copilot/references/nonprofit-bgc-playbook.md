## BGC Segment Overview

- **Avg sales cycle:** 6-18 months (lean committees, mission-scrutinized)

## Primary Persona: Executive Director / CEO

The ED is the single most important relationship in a BGC deal.
- Makes or breaks vendor decisions
- Champions technology to the board
- Cares about: youth outcomes, operational efficiency, donor confidence, grant compliance
- Speaks in: impact numbers (youth served, programs delivered, outcomes achieved)

## BGC-Specific ICP Notes

### What Makes a BGC a Good Fit
- Hundreds to thousands of youth across multiple sites
- Year-round programming (after-school, summer, sports, STEM, arts)
- Active attendance, enrollment, and outcomes reporting requirements
- Active fundraising with grant compliance needs
- Currently using manual processes or fragmented point solutions

### Account Tiers

| Tier | Profile | Approach |
|---|---|---|
| **Large Multi-Site (5+ sites, thousands of youth)** | Enterprise features needed, multiple program lines, dedicated operations and development staff | Executive engagement, peer proof from comparable BGCs, board-ready ROI materials, multi-stakeholder demos |
| **Mid-sized (1–4 sites)** | Core participant tracking and donor management priority; ED-led decisions | Pragmatic ROI case, peer proof, faster sales cycle once ED is convinced |
| **Small / Single-Site** | Lower priority unless clear growth plans | Lower-touch nurture; prioritize only when expansion or grant-driven growth signals are present |

### BGC Customer Advisory Board
Active in 2026 — use as enrichment signal and proof point. CAB participation signals commitment to the BGC movement and is a credible reference in executive-level BGC conversations.

### Buying Committee
- **Structure:** Centralized and lean — smaller committees than YMCA
- **Key authority:** Executive Director is the primary decision-maker
- **Board role:** Scrutinizes ROI and mission alignment, not technical details
- **Speed:** Can move faster than YMCA or JCC once ED is convinced
- **Budget reality:** Tight budgets, grant-funded, every dollar must show impact

### Enrichment Signals (for ABM targeting)
- New grants awarded (federal, state, foundation)
- New ED appointment (new leadership = new technology priorities)
- BGCA data initiatives (national push for outcomes measurement)
- Job postings for Data Manager, Outcomes Coordinator, or similar roles
- Expansion into new sites or programs

## Messaging Do's and Don'ts (BGC-Specific)
- **Do:** Lead with youth outcomes and community impact
- **Do:** Reference "Ready Now. Future Proven." positioning for existing customers
- **Do:** Emphasize grant compliance and outcomes reporting
- **Do:** Speak to the ED directly — they are the champion and decision-maker
- **Do:** Frame efficiency gains as "more time with kids, less time on admin"
- **Don't:** Pressure existing customers to migrate — this erodes trust
- **Don't:** Lead with technology features — lead with mission outcomes
- **Don't:** Assume BGCs operate like YMCAs — different scale, different budget, different urgency
- **Don't:** Ignore the board — provide board-ready ROI materials

## Competitive Positioning (BGC Context)

### Primary Differentiator (Updated 2026)
The "only Daxko is purpose-built for BGCs" claim is **no longer valid** as of 2026 — **MyClubHub** now serves the BGC market directly.

**Correct 2026 differentiator:** Daxko is ready now and can scale with BGCs across single-site and multi-club organizations. Position **readiness and scalability**, not uniqueness.

Most BGCs are still operating with one of:
- Vision (fully supported under "Ready Now. Future Proven." narrative)
- MyClubHub (emerging direct competitor)
- Generic nonprofit CRMs (Neon One, others — not built for program operations)
- Spreadsheets and manual processes
- Fragmented point solutions

Daxko's advantage: purpose-built for youth-serving nonprofit operations with attendance tracking, outcomes measurement, and donor management in one platform — **delivered today, at scale**, with the BGC Customer Advisory Board shaping the roadmap.

### vs. Neon One (BGC context)
- **Their pitch:** Nonprofit CRM with strong fundraising and donor management
- **Their weakness:** CRM-only, no operations depth, can't handle attendance, program scheduling, multi-site youth participant volume
- **Our counter:** Daxko is a full operations platform — Neon One is CRM/fundraising only. One system for ops + engagement + payments + outcomes reporting vs. requiring Neon One + a separate ops platform.
- **Trap question:** "When a youth attends an after-school program, then their family makes a donation at the year-end gala, are those two records connected in your system today — or do they live in separate databases that someone has to manually reconcile?"

### BGC Trap Question Library

| Competitor / Context | Trap Question |
|---|---|
| Vision (existing) | (No trap — narrative is partnership and readiness, not displacement) |
| MyClubHub | "What's MyClubHub's track record at scale across multi-club organisations — and what's their roadmap for outcomes reporting tied to federal grant requirements?" |
| Neon One | "When a youth attends a program and their family makes a donation, are those two records connected today — or do membership/ops and fundraising work from separate databases?" |
| Manual / spreadsheet operations | "How many staff hours per week does your team spend reconciling attendance data with funder outcome reports — and what's that costing you in grant reporting risk?" |

## 2026 National Market Campaign — "Ready Now for Boys & Girls Clubs"

> **Source:** PMM Brief — BGC Messaging and Campaign Strategy (Feb 2026, Constance Miller — PMM owner)
>
> **What this section adds:** the unified national positioning, the 5-pillar message architecture, the 3-phase GTM rollout, and the explicit "what this campaign is and is not" framing. Replaces the simpler "Ready Now. Future Proven." narrative for BGC-only audiences (Vision-specific narrative remains for Vision migration conversations).

### Core Positioning Statement (Verbatim, Approved)

> "Daxko is the nonprofit-native operations platform that is ready now to strengthen operational efficiency, financial clarity, and daily workflows for Boys & Girls Clubs across both single-site and multi-club organizations."

### Core Market POV

Boys & Girls Clubs are vital community institutions of every size. They are:
- Multi-site organizations
- Grant-funded operations
- Community anchor institutions
- Accountable to boards and BGCA
- Expected to deliver both mission outcomes and operational discipline

Technology must match that complexity.

### Five-Pillar Message Architecture

| # | Pillar | Market Message |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Operational Strength for Complex Clubs** | "Your Club's complexity is a strength. Your platform should be built for it." |
| 2 | **Financial Clarity That Boards Trust** | "Your finance team should not rely on patchwork exports. Clarity lives inside your operations platform." |
| 3 | **Engagement That Lives Inside Operations** | "When engagement is embedded in operations, growth becomes sustainable." |
| 4 | **Investment & Innovation That Scales with the Movement** | "Your platform evolves as your community evolves." |
| 5 | **Here for Your Needs — Now and Long-Term** | "Clubs are partners shaping the future of the platform." |

**Pillar 1 supporting capabilities:** multi-site organizations, secure purpose-built workflows, class scheduling, sports leagues, childcare & camps, facility access management, member management.

**Pillar 2 supporting capabilities:** daily KPI visibility, registrations/cancellations/check-ins, financial trend clarity, multi-site reporting visibility, reporting designed for nonprofit growth.

**Pillar 3 supporting capabilities:** personalized sales engagement, flexible payments & fundraising, unified member identity, integrated digital experiences.

**Pillar 4 supporting capabilities:** high-growth product investment, reusable feature-rich services, robust partner ecosystem, seamless all-in-one experience.

**Pillar 5 supporting capabilities:** dedicated support resources, ongoing product investment updates, opportunities to influence platform direction, long-term partnership alignment.

### Concrete Proof Points (Use in BGC-Facing Copy)

- **1,000+ mission-driven nonprofits** trust Daxko
- **33 Boys & Girls Clubs** using multiple Daxko solutions today
- BGC Customer Advisory Board active in 2026, shaping product direction
- 25+ years of operational experience with mission-driven organizations

### Three-Phase GTM Rollout

| Phase | Objective | Key Assets |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Movement Commitment** | Establish Daxko as a national partner to Boys & Girls Clubs | Dedicated BGC webpage, leadership POV article, Advisory Board announcement, webinar replay amplification, email campaign to all Clubs |
| **2. Operational Proof** | Demonstrate infrastructure strength | Reporting deep-dive demo, multi-site operational story, financial clarity guide, case study library |
| **3. Growth Narrative** | Shift from stability to strategic advantage | Innovation roadmap webinar, partner ecosystem spotlight, thought leadership content, expansion enablement materials |

### How Vision Fits Into the Story

Vision is positioned as: **One of many Clubs choosing to align with a scaled, growth-focused nonprofit platform.**

This campaign does NOT center migration. It centers the movement. Vision customers join a broader ecosystem serving Boys & Girls Clubs nationwide.

### What This Campaign Is and Is Not (Critical Framing)

**This campaign is NOT:**
- A defensive migration story
- A competitive attack (against MyClubHub or any other vendor)
- A narrow product launch

**This campaign IS:**
- A national commitment to Boys & Girls Clubs
- A clear signal that Daxko is investing in the future infrastructure of the movement
