## The manuscript

### 1. The engagement problem mission-driven orgs actually have

Most YMCAs and JCCs do not have an engagement problem because they lack ways to reach members. They have one
because reaching members well takes staff time that a mission-driven budget does not have. The member who
stopped coming in February, the family whose youngest just aged out of the toddler program, the donor who
gave once and never again — the signals are all in the system. Acting on them, one member at a time, is the
work nobody has time for.

This is the gap AI is actually useful for. Not louder outreach — **more relevant** outreach, at a scale a
small team cannot reach by hand.

### 2. What "AI-driven engagement" means when you strip the hype

Set aside the word "AI" for a moment and the useful version is simple: software that reads the member
behaviour you already collect — attendance, billing, program participation, communication history — and
turns it into the next sensible action. A re-engagement message to the member who has not checked in for
three weeks. A renewal nudge timed to the lifecycle moment it matters. A prioritised list for a staff member
of the accounts most likely to lapse.

Daxko builds this on a large base: **20 million members and roughly 19,000 facilities across 55 countries**,
serving **more than 3,000 mission-driven organizations** (`references/product-knowledge.md`, Proof Points —
Daxko Global Scale). Scale is not the point by itself, but it is why the behavioural patterns these tools
learn from are not guesswork.

### 3. What is real today, and what is coming

Here is where most vendor content overpromises, so this section is deliberate about what has shipped and what
has not.

**Available today.** Daxko Engage Pro AI auto-generates campaigns across SMS and email, personalises
outreach from real member behaviour, and triggers re-engagement automatically. Daxko's Digital Marketing
Services AI predicts campaign performance, generates creative, and optimises targeting and spend
(`references/product-knowledge.md`, AI Capabilities — In Production Today). Both are in production; both are
things a Y can use now.

**Coming Q3 2026.** AI Agents that engage members 24/7 across SMS, web, mobile app and voice, connected to
live operational data such as class availability and account balances; Member Intelligence 360°, which
summarises a member's whole history and surfaces churn risk and recommended next actions; and a Unified
Comms Center that puts the full member timeline in one place. These are **coming in Q3 2026**, not available
today (`references/product-knowledge.md`, AI Capabilities — Delivery Starting Q3 2026). An executive
evaluating a platform should ask to see them on a roadmap, not a live demo, until then.

*Note for the reviewer: the three further capabilities Daxko has under exploration for early 2027 are
deliberately not described in this piece. Naming them in a whitepaper would read as a promise, and they are
directional only (`references/product-knowledge.md`, Delivery Early 2027 — "do NOT position as available
today").*

### 5. How to adopt without overpromising — a short readiness checklist for executives

1. **Start where the signal already is.** Re-engagement and renewals are the fastest wins because the
   behaviour that predicts them is already in your system.
2. **Ask every vendor what has shipped versus what is on the roadmap** — and hold them to the answer. The
   difference is the difference between a tool you can use in Q3 and a promise.
3. **Keep a human on the decision.** AI should surface the at-risk member and the recommended action; a
   person should still decide how a mission-driven organization treats that member.

## Five things this example is teaching

1. **The thesis is locked and offered before any prose.** A whitepaper drafted off an unapproved outline can
   waste pages on the wrong structure. One thesis, one outline, then the draft.
2. **Delivery status is honoured to the letter.** Two capabilities as shipped, three as *coming Q3 2026*, the
   early-2027 items not claimed at all. In a whitepaper, a roadmap capability written as available today is a
   false claim to a customer — the exact defect corrected at source on 2026-09-18.
3. **Four placeholders, no invented facts.** A byline, a market stat, a quote and a URL were all wanted, and
   none is in a bundled file. None was guessed, and none was softened into a vague version — "many
   organizations are adopting AI" would have been the same invention with the edges filed off.
4. **Every real figure is cited to its file and section.** A reader can check each one against
   `references/product-knowledge.md`.
5. **One next step, phrased as a sentence to reply to** — the default brand check with Agent 9 — Brand
   Guardian, because a whitepaper is external content and skipping that review is an anti-pattern.
