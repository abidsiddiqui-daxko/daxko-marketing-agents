## Nonprofit Market

### YMCA

**Organization type:** Large community-focused nonprofits offering fitness, youth development, and community health. Organized into local associations. 2,597 YMCA locations in the US, ~750 buying units.

**Primary ICP:** Mid-sized to large YMCA associations — multi-branch or single large facility (5,000+ members).

**Personas:**
- **Core:** CEO / Executive Director · COO / VP of Operations
- **Secondary:** CFO / Finance Director · IT Director / CIO · Membership & Program Directors · Board Members

**Buying committee dynamic:** Multi-layered, consensus-driven. Major investments require CEO sponsorship and board approval. Peer influence is significant — Ys consult YMCA of the USA, regional networks, and neighboring Ys.

**Qualification signals:**
- Managing multiple branches OR single large facility with 5,000+ members
- Wide range of programs (aquatics, fitness, childcare, camps)
- Multiple revenue streams (memberships, fees, donations)
- Experiencing growth, consolidation, or post-pandemic recovery

**Account tiers:**
- **Tier 1 (Large Metro):** multi-branch, 5+ branches, enterprise functionality needed
- **Tier 2 (Mid-sized Regional):** 1–3 branches, several thousand members
- **Tier 3 (Small Community):** single facility, budget-sensitive

**Enrichment signals:**
- New CEO/COO/CIO appointments
- Facility expansions, mergers, or capital campaigns
- Strategic plans referencing digital transformation
- RFPs or job postings mentioning system integrations
- Prospects mentioning Salesforce or TractionRec evaluations *(2026 update)*

**Primary marketing motions:** ABM · Field marketing · Executive thought leadership · Peer proof and case studies.

**2026 target:** 65% market share — 55 of 85 YMCAs with revenue >$20M.

### JCC (Jewish Community Centers)

**Organization type:** Community centers rooted in Jewish heritage, serving diverse populations. ~150–200+ JCCs nationally via JCC Association of North America. Mid-sized to large single-site operations.

**Primary ICP:** Mid-sized to large JCCs with diverse programming — fitness, preschool, camps, cultural arts, and senior services.

**Personas:**
- **Core:** Executive Director · Operations Director
- **Secondary:** Finance Director · Board Members (particularly for capital investment)

**Buying committee dynamic:** Decision-making centered around Executive Director with Finance Director and Board. Many JCCs rely on peer guidance and external consultants due to limited IT resources.

**Qualification signals:**
- Active fitness centre and structured membership model
- Multiple overlapping programs (preschool, camps, arts, wellness)
- Growing or modernising facilities
- Increasing focus on donor engagement and fundraising
- Accumulated point solutions creating administrative friction
- Currently evaluating Amilia *(2026 update — priority displacement opportunity)*

**Account tiers:**
- Large Urban JCCs: high complexity, enterprise expectations
- Mid-sized Community JCCs: balanced needs, ROI-focused
- Smaller or Niche Centers: lower priority unless growth-oriented

**Market context:** No single technology provider has successfully built a solution tailored specifically for JCCs — this is a market opportunity. JCCs often perceive Daxko as outdated. Key competitors: TractionRec, Amilia, Xplor.

**2026 watch:**
- Amilia threat elevated — active FUD counter-campaign at daxko.com/considering-amilia
- TractionRec growing JCC adoption via Salesforce Agentforce AI
- Migration proof points: Rochester JCC, Siegel JCC

### Boys & Girls Clubs (BGC)

**Organization type:** Youth development nonprofits — after-school and summer programs. 4,000–5,500+ club sites, **~850 independent local BGC organizations** (corrected from prior 1,100 figure). Members are youth participants, not adult fitness members.

**Primary ICP:** Mid-to-large BGC organizations managing multiple sites and youth participant volume — especially those under pressure to report outcomes to funders.

**Personas:**
- **Core:** Executive Director / CEO
- **Secondary:** Board of Directors · Operations / Club Services Director · Development / Fundraising Director

**Buying committee dynamic:** Centralized and lean — Executive Director is key authority. Board scrutinizes ROI and mission alignment. BGCA national office influences data collection standards indirectly.

**Qualification signals:**
- Managing hundreds to thousands of youth annually across multiple sites
- Year-round programs (after-school, summer, sports, teens)
- Managing attendance, enrollment, and outcomes reporting for funders
- Actively fundraising through grants and corporate partnerships
- Currently on Vision OR MyClubHub *(2026 update — both are transition contexts)*

**Account tiers:**
- Large Multi-Site (5+ sites, thousands of youth): enterprise features needed
- Mid-sized (1–4 sites): core participant tracking and donor management
- Small / Single-Site: lower priority unless clear growth plans

**Enrichment signals:**
- New large grants or donations
- New Executive Director appointment
- BGCA-driven initiatives requiring enhanced data collection
- Job postings: 'Data Manager', 'Outcomes Coordinator', 'Development Manager'
- BGC Customer Advisory Board membership *(active — use as proof point)*

**2026 positioning rules:**
- Campaign theme: **"Ready Now for Club Growth"** — movement-centered, not migration-centered
- "Only purpose-built for BGCs" claim is **deprecated** — MyClubHub now serves the segment
- Correct differentiator: Daxko is ready now and can scale across single-site and multi-club organizations
- Do **not** position Daxko as a Vision replacement
- 2026 target: **229 customers (27% of 850 buying universe)**

### Community Centers

**Organization type:** Independent nonprofit community centers, municipal/county recreation centers, and faith-based centers — not affiliated with YMCA, JCC, or BGC networks.

**Primary ICP:** Multi-program nonprofit or municipal community centers managing registration, scheduling, payments, and program reporting.

**Personas:**
- **Core:** Executive Director / Centre Director · Parks & Recreation Director

**Buying committee dynamics:**
- Nonprofit centres: Executive Director + Board
- Municipal centres: Parks & Rec Director + City IT/Procurement
- Usability for non-technical staff and demonstrable community value are key drivers

**Market size:** 3,490 independent community recreation centres in US — $2B+ annual revenue, 52,882 employees.

**Qualification signals:**
- Multiple programs year-round
- Hundreds or thousands of participants annually
- Scheduling, registration, and payment processing required
- Must report to funders, boards, or city councils
- Replacing outdated desktop software, paper forms, or spreadsheets

**Lead proof point — Edgar May Community Center:** After switching to Daxko: +41% registrations, +314% donations, +11% memberships.

**Top competitors:**
- Tier 1 small: RecDesk, MyRec.com, EZFacility
- Tier 2 mid-sized: ACTIVENet, CivicRec, Amilia
- Tier 3 large: RecTrac (strongest), Xplor Recreation

**2026 target:** ~$1.03M new logo pipeline.

## Club Market

**Market definition:** For-profit fitness and wellness operators — single-location gyms to large multi-site health clubs and franchise networks. Revenue-driven, operationally complex, focused on member retention and revenue per square foot.

**Approved Club segments:** Fitness · Multi-Purpose · Campus Recreation · Corporate Wellness · Country Club · HOA · Hotel · Medical Fitness & Recovery · Eatertainment (emerging) · Racquet

**Primary products:**
- Club Automation (gyms and health clubs)
- Motionsoft (enterprise / multi-location)
- Engage Pro (lead management and marketing automation)

### Segment 1 — Single-Location Health Club / Gym

**Profile:** Single location, 500–5,000 members, for-profit, community gym or boutique health club.

**Personas:**
- **Primary buyer:** Club Owner / Operator — makes all technology decisions, focused on ROI and revenue growth
- **Secondary:** General Manager · Membership Sales Director · Finance / Billing Manager

**Core pain points:**
- Manual billing and payment failures draining staff time
- Member churn with no early warning signals
- Scattered tech stack — scheduling in one tool, billing in another
- Difficulty converting leads to members (no automated follow-up)

**Key buying triggers:**
- Opening a new location
- Outgrowing current software
- High card decline rates
- Staff complaining about manual admin
- Launching digital membership sales

**Decision-making dynamics:**
- Owner is the decision maker
- 3–6 month sales cycle
- ROI-driven
- Strong focus on implementation timeline and references from similar-sized clubs

**Key metrics:** Revenue per member · Member acquisition cost · Card decline rate · Churn / retention rate · Staff productivity.

### Segment 2 — Multi-Location Fitness Operator

**Profile:** 2–50+ locations, regional or national footprint, 5,000–50,000+ members, may be franchise or corporate-owned.

**Personas:**
- **Primary buyer:** VP of Operations / COO — responsible for standardizing operations across locations
- **Secondary:** CFO / Finance Director · IT Director · Membership Sales Director

**Core pain points:**
- Inconsistent member experience across locations
- No consolidated reporting across sites
- Complex corporate membership management
- High implementation and migration risk

**Key buying triggers:**
- Acquiring a new club brand
- Franchise network standardization initiative
- Current system unable to scale
- Board or investor pressure to improve financial reporting

**Decision-making dynamics:**
- Committee-based, 6–12 month sales cycle
- Requires C-suite sign-off
- References from comparable multi-site operators critical
- Implementation timeline and risk management are major concerns

### Segment 3 — Medical Fitness & Specialty Wellness

**Profile:** Often affiliated with a hospital or healthcare system. Mission-driven but for-profit or quasi-public. Serves rehabilitation, chronic disease management, and adaptive athletes.

**Personas:**
- **Primary buyer:** Director of Wellness / Director of Fitness — accountable to healthcare system leadership
- **Secondary:** CFO · IT Director · Development / Fundraising Director (if nonprofit-adjacent)

**Core pain points:**
- Managing complex membership categories (patients, staff, community)
- Integration with healthcare systems
- Outcome reporting for accreditation and funders
- Adaptive equipment and program scheduling complexity

**Proof point — Lakeshore Foundation (Birmingham, AL):**
- 45-acre adaptive fitness campus
- Home to 3 US Paralympic teams
- Uses Club Automation for operations, membership, and program management
- 400+ athletes enrolled in first 18 months of Sports Science & Performance Center
- $1M/year x 3 year fundraising initiative

**Competitive context:** Jonas Fitness has historically been strong in this segment (YMCAs, hospital wellness). Club Automation differentiates with cloud reliability (99.9998% delivered uptime vs. Jonas downtime), automated billing, native mobile app, and modern UX.

### Segment 1 — Independent Gyms & Studios

**Profile:** Core services — open gym, group classes, personal training. Members: 50–500. Staff: 2–10. Owner-operated, community-driven, budget-conscious.

### Segment 2 — Growth-Oriented Small Chains (2–10 sites)

**Profile:** Core services — multi-site membership, group fitness, PT, retail. Members: 1K–5K. Staff: 15–60. Expansion mindset, need for standardized operations and reporting.

### Segment 3 — Martial Arts Academies

**Profile:** Belt programs, camps, tournaments. Students: 100–600. Staff: 1–8. Program-centric billing, attendance tracking, parents as buyers.

### Segment 4 — Fitness Creators / Influencers (NEW for 2026)

**Profile:** Workout plans for sale direct to consumer, custom branded app, challenges, memberships. Served by Exercise.com.

### Segment 5 — Personal Trainers (NEW for 2026)

**Profile:** Client management, program delivery, online coaching. Served by Exercise.com.

### SugarWOD Integration ICP (CrossFit-specific)

**Primary segment:** CrossFit affiliates
**Membership size:** 75–500 active members
**Staff size:** 3–15 coaches and administrators

**Pain points:**
- Using whiteboards for workouts
- Manual spreadsheet tracking
- Disengaged members
- Administrative double-entry between applications

**Qualification signals:**
- Social media posts asking for better workout-tracking apps
- High athlete churn
- Spreadsheets for leaderboards
- Stated interest in a unified 'one app' experience

**Integration benefits:**
1. Single Member Record — attendance, billing, and workout performance data synced automatically
2. Enhanced Member Engagement — push notifications, leaderboards, and social features drive more frequent visits and retention
3. Improved Coach Efficiency — programming published once, scores and results flow directly into Zen Planner reports
4. New Upsell Pathways — performance data identifies opportunities for PT packages or specialty programs

### Boutique Buying Committee

| Role | Function |
|------|----------|
| Owner / CEO | Final sign-off — focuses on ROI and risk |
| General Manager / Head Coach | Day-to-day operations — pushes for efficiency and member experience |
| Marketing Lead / Front-Desk Supervisor | Evaluates marketing automation and ease of use |
| Bookkeeper / Finance Admin | Validates billing accuracy, fees, and reporting |

**Typical decision flow:** Owner researches in peer groups → short-lists competitors (PushPress, Wodify) → free trial or demo → decision within 7–14 days.

**Sales cycle:** Days to weeks — very fast. Owner is the decision maker. High price sensitivity. Word of mouth and community referrals matter.

**Strong fit:**

- Opening second location or adding new programs
