## Segment Intelligence

### Zen Planner — Martial Arts (Primary from Q3 2026)
- **Verticals:** BJJ, MMA, boxing, karate, TKD, Krav Maga
- **Profile:** 100-600 students, belt programs, camps, tournaments
- **Buyers:** Studio owner (often head instructor) · Parents (for youth programs)
- **Pain:** Belt progression tracking, family billing (kids + adults), attendance tracking, scheduling complexity

- **Buying cycle:** Days to weeks. Owner researches in peer groups → short-lists (PushPress, Wodify) → demo → decision 7-14 days.

### Exercise.com — Functional Fitness (Primary from Q3 2026)
- **Verticals:** CrossFit, functional fitness, HIIT studios, personal trainers, fitness influencers, online coaching
- **Profile:** Individual coaches, trainers, and influencers building digital training businesses
- **Buyers:** Owner/CEO, independent coach, fitness influencer
- **Key features:** Custom branded apps, workout plans for sale, challenges, memberships, online coaching platform
- **Competitors:** Gymdesk, Glofox, Vagaro
- **Win theme:** Exercise.com custom branded apps + full business platform

- **Segment:** CrossFit affiliates, 75-500 active members, 3-15 coaches
- **Pain:** Whiteboard tracking, manual spreadsheets, disengaged members, admin double-entry

## Competitive Intelligence (Boutique)

### PushPress — Primary Threat
- **Strengths:** Strong UX, frequent releases, active community, $20M Series B, AI Assistant in beta 2026, simple low-cost entry (free plan)
- **Weaknesses:** Community forum DIY support only (no dedicated 1:1 onboarding) · No Campaign Marketplace · No native Facebook/Google Ads · SugarWOD (Train) is separate, separately priced add-on
- **Win when:** Advanced reporting needed · Integrated access control · Uses/wants SugarWOD · Needs digital advertising · Growing past entry-level · Burned by DIY support
- **Lose when:** Free entry-level plan preferred by startups · Absolute simplicity wanted · PushPress AI vision excites the prospect · ZP Capterra reviews (bugs, slowness) already seen by prospect · G2 perception gap (PushPress rated better on ongoing product support)
- **Trap questions:** "When a member asks what app to use to track workouts and PRs — what does PushPress give you to answer that?" / "Walk me through how you'd run a Facebook ad campaign from inside PushPress today."
- ⚠️ **ZP vulnerability:** ZP Capterra reviews (bugs, slow system, registration issues) are visible and referenced. G2 shows PushPress rated better on product support. Design campaigns that address this proactively — "we heard you" messaging.

### Wodify
- **Strengths:** Deep CrossFit credibility, AI churn prediction, improved 30-60-90 day onboarding, $79/month vs. ZP $99, native workout tracking + leaderboards
- **Weaknesses:** Retention-only platform — no new client acquisition tools · No Campaign Marketplace · No native digital ads · Belt tracking unusable for martial arts (documented Trustpilot)
- **Win when:** Multi-vertical gym (ZP covers MA + functional fitness; Wodify = CrossFit-first) · New client acquisition needed · Digital ads needed · Campaign Marketplace needed · Belt tracking for MA needed
- **Lose when:** Deep CrossFit community and already knows Wodify · Improved Wodify onboarding erodes ZP's advantage · Pricing perception ($79 vs. $99) · Wants native workout tracking without SugarWOD subscription
- **Trap question:** "If you bring in 20 new Instagram leads this month — show me how Wodify converts them into paid members. What's automated, what's manual?"

### Mindbody (Boutique)
- **Strengths:** Consumer marketplace (3.5M+ active users) · ClassPass ownership (now Mindbody-owned) · Multi-location enterprise scale
- **Weaknesses:** Removed all pricing from website 2026 ("talk to sales" + $139-599+ opaque + 20% marketplace fee) · "Built for salons" not gyms · Support quality issues documented · Steep implementation curve
- **Win when:** Price shock (ZP $99/month transparent) · "Just a number" support experience · Purpose-built vs. generic · Needs SugarWOD equivalent · Complex onboarding comparison
- **Lose when:** Gym relies on Mindbody consumer app for discovery · ClassPass dependency · Multi-location enterprise growth ambitions · Switching inertia
- **Trap questions:** "When you called support about belt tracking / WOD programming — did they know what you were talking about?" / "If you turned off the Mindbody consumer app tomorrow, how would you replace those leads — and what's your plan when they raise the fee?"

### WellnessLiving
- **Strengths:** ~$69/month entry price · Aggressive Mindbody alternative positioning · Consumer booking app
- **Weaknesses:** Built for yoga/spa/dance/salons — limited MA/CrossFit depth · Hidden fees, contract auto-renewals, cancellation pain (documented Capterra) · Payment processing friction (Paragon) · Fees can change without notice
- **Win when:** Fitness-specific depth matters · SugarWOD wanted · Campaign Marketplace comparison · Prospect burned by auto-renewal or hidden fees
- **Lose when:** Entry-level price ($69 vs. ZP $99) · Prospect fleeing Mindbody on price only · Yoga/Pilates-adjacent studios (better WL fit) · Canadian/international markets
- **Trap questions:** "When your members ask what app to track PRs and WODs — what does WellnessLiving give you?" / "Have you priced out what WellnessLiving's payment processing actually costs per transaction?"

### Kicksite / Spark Membership — Martial Arts Vertical
- **Positioning:** Martial-arts-specific point solutions. Kicksite focuses on dojo/academy management; Spark Membership targets martial arts and youth programs with low-cost entry.
- **Strengths:** Vertical familiarity (martial arts owners recognize them) · Low entry price · Simple workflows
- **Weaknesses vs. ZP:** Narrow platform (no functional fitness, no full marketing automation) · No Campaign Marketplace · No Engage AI suite · No SugarWOD-equivalent · Limited reporting depth
- **Win when:** Prospect wants the full Daxko platform (Campaign Marketplace, Engage AI, Flex Fees, Revenue Recovery) · Multi-program studio (MA + youth + camps + tournaments) · Owner is growth-oriented, not cost-floor-shopping
- **Lose when:** Owner wants the absolute cheapest MA-specific tool · No appetite for full platform · Strong existing referral relationship with Kicksite or Spark community
- **Trap question:** "When you bring in 20 new leads this month — show me how Kicksite/Spark turns them into long-term students with automated nurture, review requests, and missed-call recovery. What's automated, what's manual?"

### 1club.ai — Emerging AI-Native Threat
- **Positioning:** AI-native gym management software built from scratch with no legacy architecture. Attacking all incumbents (ZP, PushPress, Wodify, Mindbody) from a clean slate.
- **Strengths:** Clean slate AI architecture (no bolted-on AI) · Marketing narrative resonates with owners frustrated by legacy software · Likely lower price point as new entrant
- **Weaknesses (current):** Unproven at scale · No SugarWOD-equivalent · No established support model · No multi-vertical depth · No published customer base
- **Win when:** Prospect needs purpose-built MA or functional fitness depth · Wants proven 80k+ review integration (SugarWOD) · Cares about support quality (1:1 onboarding vs. unknown) · Has had AI-tool fatigue and needs operational reliability
- **Lose when:** Prospect is excited by "AI-first from scratch" narrative · Cost-sensitive startup · Already evaluating multiple AI-native tools
- **Monitor:** Track 1club.ai customer announcements, pricing, partnerships through 2026. This is a category-disruption threat, not a feature-parity threat.

### AI Objection Handling (confirmed talk tracks)
- **"Is this replacing staff?"** — No. It's reducing manual work so staff can focus on member experience and growth.
- **"Is our data safe?"** — Yes. AI operates inside the same secure environment that already protects member identity, payments, and permissions.
- **"We already use AI partners."** — That's great. ZP is designed to support that. Over time, native capabilities may reduce overlap, but compatibility and choice remain.

### Boutique-Specific AI Talk Tracks
- **Reviews AI:** "Your members are happy — they just forget to leave a review. Reviews AI asks at the right moment, lifts your Google rating, and shows up the next time someone searches 'BJJ near me.'"
- **Voice AI:** "How many leads do you lose because the phone rings while you're teaching? Voice AI answers, captures, and books them — so you never trade a class for a missed call."

### AI Guardrails (Boutique)
- **Always emphasize:** Trust, security, and choice · Operational relief · Time back on the mat / floor
- **Avoid:** Replacement narratives · "One AI tool to rule them all" · Minimizing partner value
- **Voice rule:** If AI sounds like a feature to sell instead of work getting done, the message is off-voice

## Campaign Strategy Guardrails

### Always
1. Speak to the passion and purpose behind the business — owners started for love, not money
2. Respect that owners wear many hats — emphasize simplicity and time savings
3. Use social proof from similar businesses — peer validation drives decisions in Boutique
4. Highlight quick wins and immediate value — show ROI in days, not months
5. Speak owner-to-owner — passionate operators who built something personal
6. Quantify time saved ("Get 5 hours/week back from admin to spend on the floor/mat")
7. Route correct segment to correct product (MA → ZP; functional fitness → Exercise.com)
8. Use full product names: "Zen Planner" not "ZP"; "Exercise.com"; "SugarWOD" in full

### Never
- Corporate enterprise language — not "stakeholders," these are studio owners
- "Just a gym" or "small business" dismissively
- Lead with features — lead with the life they get back
- One-size-fits-all — martial arts ≠ CrossFit ≠ personal trainer. Different cultures, different language.
- Aggressive growth messaging — many chose lifestyle businesses intentionally
- Yoga/Pilates content — not a target vertical
- Imply or promise a ZP free trial
- Donor outreach or fundraising AI — not applicable to Boutique

## Anti-Patterns

**Enterprise framing:**
> "Daxko's enterprise-grade member management platform enables boutique fitness operators to optimize their operational workflows..."
→ Better: "You didn't open a martial arts studio to spend 10 hours a week on billing and scheduling. Here's how to get that time back on the mat."

**One-size-fits-all boutique:**
> Same campaign for CrossFit boxes and BJJ academies.
→ Completely different cultures, pain points, language. A CrossFit owner talks about PRs and WOD leaderboards. A BJJ instructor talks about belt rank, family tuition, and mat hours.

**Acquisition-first:**
> "Grow your studio to 500 members with Daxko's marketing automation."
→ Many boutique owners don't want 500 members. They want the 150 they have to stay longer and pay more. Lead with retention.
→ Better: "When Ironside BJJ cut billing failures by 80%, they stopped losing 3 members a month to payment friction they didn't even know about."

**Wrong product routing:**
> Running ZP campaigns for CrossFit boxes (Q3 2026+)
→ CrossFit/functional fitness → Exercise.com. Martial arts → ZP.

**Promising a ZP free trial:**
> "Try Zen Planner free for 30 days..."
→ No trial offering exists. Use demo path instead.

## Boutique Market Knowledge

### Studio Segments
- **Martial arts studios** — Strong community identity. Belt progression creates natural
  retention. Pain: scheduling complexity, family billing (kids + adults), attendance tracking.
  Decision maker: Studio owner (often head instructor). Sub-segments: Karate/TKD, BJJ,
  MMA, Krav Maga — each with distinct culture and terminology.
- **CrossFit / functional fitness** — High intensity, high community loyalty. Pain: class
  capacity management, member engagement outside the box, programming variety.
  Decision maker: Box owner, often a former competitor. SugarWOD integration is the
  key differentiator here (80K+ reviews, 4.9 stars on Apple).
  management, membership flexibility (class packs vs unlimited). Decision maker: Studio owner.
- **Personal training** — Client management and program delivery. Pain: scaling beyond
  in-person, tracking client progress, online coaching setup. Decision maker: Individual trainer.
- **Franchise boutique (F45, OrangeTheory, etc.)** — Franchisor-driven tech decisions.
  Pain: brand compliance across locations, centralized reporting, member transfers.
  Decision maker: Franchisee owner, influenced by corporate recommendations.

### Vertical Nuances

| Vertical | Key Features | Community Driver | Revenue Model |
|----------|-------------|-----------------|---------------|
| Martial Arts | Belt progression, family memberships, tournaments | Rank advancement, loyalty | Monthly dues + belt test fees + tournament fees |
| CrossFit/Functional Fitness | WOD tracking, community leaderboards, competitions | Leaderboards, team challenges | Monthly membership + drop-ins + competitions |
| Personal Training | Client management, program delivery, online coaching | 1:1 accountability | Session packages + monthly retainers + online programs |

### Key Metrics
- **Member churn rate** — The north star. Reducing churn is the fastest path to growth
- **Member penetration** — What % of local market potential is captured
- **Class fill rate** — Empty spots = lost revenue. Full classes = waitlist pressure
- **Trial-to-member conversion** — How effectively trials become paying members
- **Revenue per member** — Add-ons, merchandise, workshops beyond base membership

### Pain Points (ranked by urgency)
1. Member churn — losing members faster than gaining them
2. Manual administrative burden — owner doing everything by hand
3. No visibility into business health — gut feel instead of data
4. Poor member experience — clunky booking, billing friction
5. Can't compete with big-box on technology or convenience
