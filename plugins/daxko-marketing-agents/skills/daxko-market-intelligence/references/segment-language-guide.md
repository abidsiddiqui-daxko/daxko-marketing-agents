**Segment Language Guide**

YMCA, JCC, and Boys & Girls Clubs

Created – Oct 2026

---

**Purpose:** Keep every customer- and prospect-facing asset in the language each nonprofit segment uses for itself. Load this guide alongside the Daxko Nonprofit Copywriter skill any time copy goes to one or more of these segments.

**Why it exists:** A Y-specific email reached an audience that includes JCCs and Boys & Girls Clubs. Sales reports that both groups already feel Daxko focuses on Ys. Word choice is one of the fastest ways to confirm or disprove that feeling.

**Status:** v1 draft built from each segment’s own published brand and style guidance, plus how member organizations write about themselves (websites, job postings, handbooks). Items marked **[Validate]** need confirmation from Sales, CS, or customers before they become hard rules. See Part 7.
# Part 1: The Five Rules
1. **Pick the segment before you write.** If you cannot name the segment, you are not ready to write. The segment field on the ticket is the source of truth. If the ticket says “No Specific Segment,” or names more than one segment, write neutral copy (Rule 4).

2. **Use the segment’s word for the thing.** Location, leader, person served, and program area all change by segment (see Part 2).

3. **Never borrow another segment’s brand phrases.** Y mission language, BGCA taglines, and JCC brand pillars belong to those organizations.

4. **If an asset goes to more than one segment, go neutral.** No segment nouns, no segment examples, no Y default.

5. **Balance proof and visuals, not just words.** A Y-only case study, Y-only logo wall, or Y-only screenshot reads as “Daxko is for Ys,” even when the copy is clean.

# Part 3: Segment Profiles
## YMCA
### Say
- “Your Y,” “your branches,” “your association” (to Y executives only)

- members, program participants, families, kids, teens, campers

- “Strengthen community” as the Y’s own purpose language, when quoting or echoing a customer

- Plural: “Ys” or “YMCAs”
### Notes
- Y-USA’s own messaging guide tells Ys to use plain language and avoid internal jargon in public-facing copy, including the word “association.” Because Daxko writes to Y professionals, “association” and “branch” are correct and expected here. They are wrong for every other segment.

- Y-USA guidance favors framing around impact (what changes for people) over services provided. That matches the Mission Payoff framework already in the skill.
### Avoid
- Calling the Y “a cause” (Y-USA’s testing found outside audiences do not see it that way)

- Treating Y program names (for example, Achievers, Healthy Kids Day) as generic terms 

- Referring to single YMCA locations as a “gym” or “club” and members as “customers” or “clients”. 

## JCC
### Say
- “Your JCC,” “your center,” “your campus,” “your members and families”

- “JCC Movement” for the collective

- “JCCs and JCC Camps” when referring to the network (JCC Association’s own phrasing)

- “Open to all” is a core JCC message: JCCs serve many people beyond the Jewish community, and the national organization reports roughly half a million friends and neighbors engaging each week. Never write as if members are all Jewish.
### Notes
- JCC Association asks that references stay broad enough for U.S. and Canadian JCCs: prefer “community” or “continental” over “national” or “American” unless a specific U.S. fact is meant.

- The same guidance asks writers to explain acronyms, translate Hebrew and other non-English words on first use, use people-first disability language, avoid euphemisms like “differently abled,” and avoid victim framing (“suffers from,” “confined to a wheelchair”). Treat these as baseline for JCC copy and good practice everywhere.

- Many JCCs partner closely with lay leaders and a board, and are supported by their local Jewish Federation. Decision-making language that assumes a single buyer may miss.

- Do not assume fitness is the primary lens. Early childhood, camp, arts and culture, and Jewish life carry equal weight for many JCCs.
### Avoid
- “Branch” and “association” (see Part 4)

- Using Jewish terms, holidays, or Hebrew as decoration. Use them only when the customer does, and explain on first use.

- “JCCA” and “the JCC Association”
## Boys & Girls Clubs
### Say
- “Your Club” or “your Clubs” (always capitalized), “Clubhouse,” “Club members,” “Club staff”

- youth, young people, kids, teens

- “Boys & Girls Clubs” with the ampersand, as a singular noun

- “Unit” and “site” when the topic is operations (for example, “each unit’s attendance”)
### Notes
- BGCA’s own style guide capitalizes Club and Clubs everywhere, including when used alone. It writes “member” lowercase (“Club member”), and capitalizes “Club Experience” when it is used as BGCA’s umbrella term.

- BGCA brands teen spaces separately (“The CLUB,” for ages 13 to 18). Do not write “teen program” copy as if it were the same as the main youth experience without checking.

- Child privacy is a hard line. BGCA guidance is to use first names only for youth under 18. Never use full names of minors in case studies, testimonials, or examples unless a release and org approval are confirmed.

- Donors and funders sit behind most BGC decisions. Outcomes language belongs here more than in the other two segments.

- BGCA keeps its brand out of political alignment. Do not tie product or campaign copy to political issues.
### Avoid
- “Branch” as a default. Some local BGCs use it informally (we found it in a few job postings), but BGCA’s standard is Club. **[Validate]** with BGC customers.

- “the Boys & Girls Clubs,” “Boys and Girls Clubs,” lowercase “club” for a location

- “Great Futures Start Here,” “Whatever It Takes,” or Blue Door imagery in any Daxko copy. These are BGCA trademarks and campaigns.
# Part 4: Words to Scrub From Non-Y Copy


| **Word or phrase** | **Why it fails outside the Y** | **Use instead** |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **branch** | Y-specific location term | JCC: center / campus. BGC: Club / Clubhouse. Neutral: location |
| **association (as in “your association”)** | Y insider term. For JCCs it points at JCC Association, the national body. | “your organization,” “your JCC,” “your Boys & Girls Clubs.” Exception: when an account’s own legal name contains “Association” (a YM-YWHA), use their name as written — never “association” as a common noun for a JCC. |
| **the Y, Ys, Y members, Y staff** | Y-specific | “members,” “staff,” or the segment’s own term |
| **Y program names** | Y-owned | The activity itself (“day camp,” “afterschool”) |
| **“strengthening** **community,” “potential, purpose, and each other,” “for a better us”** | Y brand language | Describe the outcome in your own words |
| **Executive Director as the default title** | Not the top title in every segment | Match the segment (see Part 2) |
| **nonprofit as a stand-in for “Y”** | Reads as Y-by-default | Name the segments |
# Part 5: Before and After
*All examples below are illustrative drafts, not approved copy.*
### Operations message (Y-specific, as it might slip through)
*Give every branch in your association one view of member engagement.*

| **Segment** | **Rewrite** |
| --- | --- |
| **YMCA** | Give every branch in your association one view of member engagement. |
| **JCC** | Give every program at your JCC, from early childhood to fitness to camp, one view of member engagement. |
| **BGC** | Give every Club in your organization one view of member attendance and youth outcomes. |
| **Neutral** | Give every location in your organization one view of member engagement. |
### Social proof line
*Trusted by YMCAs nationwide.*

- **Mixed audience:** “Trusted by YMCAs, JCCs, and Boys & Girls Clubs.” Rotate the order across sends so no segment is always first.

- **Segment** **send:** lead with that segment’s customer, or with a verified number covering all segments.

- **Only one segment’s proof available:** say what it is (“See how one YMCA did it”) and do not generalize it.
### Mission payoff
*Hours back to your staff means more time for your members.*

Works as is for Y and JCC. For BGC, swap in “youth” and add the outcome funders care about: “Hours back to your Club staff means more time with youth and clearer outcomes to share with your funders.”
# Part 6: Pre-Send Checklist
Run before any send, publish, or handoff to MOps. Tie it to the market segment field on the ticket.

☐  Segment confirmed on the ticket, and the copy matches it

☐  Location, leader, and person-served words match the quick reference table

☐  No Y-specific nouns (branch, association, the Y) in non-Y copy

☐  No borrowed brand phrases or taglines from any national body

☐  Mixed-segment asset: all segment nouns stripped, examples balanced, segment order rotated

☐  Case study or proof point: segment-matched, approved, and not presented as representative of other segments

☐  Acronyms spelled out and non-English words explained on first use (required for JCC)

☐  **Dynamic** **content and merge fields:** the fallback value is neutral, never “YMCA”

☐  Images, logos, and screenshots reflect the segment (or all three, for mixed sends)

☐  If the subject line, preview text, or sender name has a segment word, it is the right one
# Part 7: How Confident Is This, and How to Make It Better
### What public sources settle
- How each national body wants its own name written (Y vs. YMCA, JCC Association phrasing, BGCA ampersand and capitalization rules)

- The documented vocabulary of each network (branch/association, Club/Unit, JCC Movement)

- Inclusion and privacy language expectations (JCC Association, BGCA)
### What public sources do not settle
- What your specific buyers find irritating, and how strongly. Sales’ report that BGCs and JCCs feel Y-centric is real signal, but we have no data on which words or visuals trigger it.

- Local variation. Some BGCs say “branch,” some JCCs say “branches” for satellite sites, some Ys may avoid “association.” Public documents show usage, not preference.

- Leader titles by account, and whether the person you are writing to uses the title in the table.

- Daxko-specific terms. If Daxko Operations labels a location “branch” in the product UI, customers in other segments see it every day, and copy cannot fix it alone. Check the product.
### Build-out plan (in rough order of effort)
1. **Product and asset audit (1 to 2 days).** Search product UI labels, help center, templates, past emails, landing pages, and sales decks for “branch,” “association,” “Y,” and Y-only proof. Count and rank. This tells you where the problem actually lives.

2. **Sales input.** What do prospects and customers call their locations, leaders, and the people they serve?

3. **Call and ticket mining.** Search Gong transcripts, support tickets, and onboarding notes by segment for how customers describe their own organizations. Customers’ own words are the best source of truth.

4. **Name an owner and a refresh cadence.** Quarterly review with PMM and Sales is enough. Use the same group that owns the segment-communications process.

# **YWCA Quick Reference**
YWCA is not one of the three main segments, so it lives here instead of in the main table. It is a separate organization from the YMCA, not a Y sub-brand. YWCA USA is a national membership organization of more than 190 independent Local Associations.

**Why it matters:** “the Y” is a nickname both organizations have used, which has caused confusion, and some local YWCAs and YMCAs have merged. Confirm an account’s actual name before choosing a column, and never send Y-specific copy to a YWCA by default.

| **Concept** | **YWCA** |
| --- | --- |
| **How they name themselves** | “YWCA,” with no “the.” Follow each local org’s exact spelling (for example, YWCA Seattle | King | Snohomish). Never “the Y.” One local YWCA’s guidelines allow “YW” as shorthand and say “The Y” refers to YMCA. That is one association’s rule, not a national one. **[Validate]** |
| **A single location** | No standard term found. Child care pages use centers, academies, and “locations” (Early Education Centers, Early Learning Academies, shelters). Use the program’s own name, or “site” as a neutral fallback. **[Validate]** |
| **The legal entity** | **Local Association.** Each is a fully independent 501(c)(3) with its own leadership, bylaws, budget, and board. |
| **National body** | YWCA USA. It is affiliated with World YWCA, a global movement in more than 120 countries. |
| **Network** | “YWCA network” or “nationwide network of Local Associations.” YWCA’s own pages use “movement” for World YWCA, not for the U.S. network. |
| **People served** | Women, girls, and communities of color are the stated focus. Their pages also say families, kids, parents, survivors, and clients. “Members” is not the default, since YWCA USA calls the Local Associations its member organizations. **[Validate]** |
| **Top leader** | Not confirmed. Check per account. **[Validate]** |
| **Site leader** | Not confirmed. Check per account. **[Validate]** |
| **Program frame** | Three Areas of Focus: Racial Justice & Civil Rights, Empowerment & Economic Advancement, and Health & Safety. Child care sits under Health & Safety: child care, Head Start, Pre-K, after-school, summer camp, and enrichment. |
| **Values frame** | Eliminating racism, empowering women, and promoting peace, justice, freedom, and dignity for all. |
 
### **Notes**
1. **Advocacy stance:** YWCA publicly advocates on policy issues, including racial justice, reproductive justice, and economic security. That is the opposite of BGCA, which keeps its brand out of politics. Do not borrow or echo these positions in Daxko copy, and do not write copy that conflicts with the mission language.

2. **Faith:** YWCA USA’s 2015 name change moved away from Christian framing. Do not reference the old name or any faith language.

3. **Domestic violence programs:** many Local Associations run shelters and survivor services. Treat client details, case examples, and locations as sensitive, and never use them as testimonial material without approval. This is an inference from the program type. **[Validate]**

4. **Child care overlap:** likely the closest product fit for Alaris-type messaging. YWCA’s child care language centers on early learning, school readiness, and enabling parents to work.

5. **“Clients”:** YWCA’s own pages use “clients” for service programs, so a Y-copy guardrail against “clients” should not be applied here.
### **Avoid**
1. “The Y,” “Ys,” Y mission language, and Y program names (for example, Child Watch)

2. Treating YWCA as a Y sub-brand or a women’s version of anything

3. “Branch” by default, and “The YWCA” (use “YWCA”)
### **Add to the checklist and validation list**
1. Pre-send check: account confirmed as YMCA, YWCA, dual-name, or YM-YWHA (some Js operate as YM-YWHAs) before choosing language

2. Pre-send check: the CRM org-type field separates YWCA from YMCA, and the merge-field fallback never says “YMCA” for a YWCA record

3. Validate: how many YWCAs are in the database and pipeline, and whether they are tagged as the YMCA segment today

4. Validate: which Daxko products YWCAs use, leader titles, the site-leader term, and whether “clients,” “participants,” or “members” is right
