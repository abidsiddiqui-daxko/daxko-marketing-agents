## JCC Segment Overview
- **Estimated addressable market:** ~150-200+ JCCs nationally
- **Current Daxko customers:** 51 (as of Aug 2025)
- **Primary ICP:** Mid-to-large JCCs with diverse programming (fitness, preschool, camps, cultural arts, senior services)
- **Avg sales cycle:** 6-18 months (committee-driven, consultant-influenced)

## JCC-Specific ICP Notes

### What Makes a JCC a Good Fit
- Active fitness center (not just cultural programming)
- Multiple overlapping programs: membership + camps + preschool + cultural arts + senior services
- Growing or actively modernizing operations
- Donor engagement is a strategic priority
- Currently running accumulated point solutions (separate systems for registration, membership, fundraising)

### Buying Committee
- **Structure:** Executive Director + Finance Director + Board
- **IT reality:** Limited dedicated IT staff — JCCs rely heavily on peer guidance and consultants for technology decisions
- **Decision dynamics:** Board scrutinizes ROI and mission alignment; ED champions the initiative
- **Peer influence:** JCC Association of North America recommendations, peer JCC referrals

### Budget Timing
- Fiscal year varies by JCC — confirm before campaign timing
- Budget decisions often tied to board meeting cycles (quarterly)
- Smaller budgets than large YMCAs — demonstrate clear ROI on every dollar

### Cultural Considerations
- Community-centered mission with interfaith and cultural programming components
- Strong emphasis on intergenerational programming (youth through senior)
- Donor relationships are deeply personal and long-standing
- Privacy and data security are heightened concerns

### JCC Account Tiers

| Tier | Profile | Approach |
|------|---------|----------|
| Large Urban JCCs | High complexity, enterprise expectations, full programming mix (fitness + camps + preschool + cultural arts + seniors) | Full platform sale; lead with operational depth and reporting; ABM-style engagement |
| Mid-sized Community JCCs | Balanced needs, ROI-focused, growing or modernizing | Demo-led demand gen; emphasize integration story and board-ready reporting |
| Smaller or Niche Centres | Lower priority unless growth-oriented | Pass unless clear growth signals (facility expansion, leadership change, donor engagement push) |

## Market Opportunity

No single vendor has fully solved JCC needs. JCCs require a unique combination of:
- Membership management (with complex family structures)
- Camp registration and management
- Preschool/early childhood enrollment
- Cultural arts and event programming
- Fundraising and donor management
- Senior services tracking

This fragmentation creates opportunity for a unified platform story.

## Perception Challenge

**Critical insight:** JCCs often perceive Daxko as outdated.

This is the #1 barrier to overcome in JCC campaigns. Every touchpoint must counter this perception:
- Lead with modern capabilities (mobile, self-service, API integrations)
- Show the integration story (one platform replacing 3-5 point solutions)
- Use fresh, modern visual assets and messaging
- Reference recent product investments and roadmap
- Provide live demos early in the sales cycle — don't rely on slide decks

## Messaging Do's and Don'ts (JCC-Specific)
- **Do:** Lead with modern capabilities and integration story (counters "outdated" perception)
- **Do:** Reference intergenerational programming and diverse community needs
- **Do:** Emphasize data unification across membership, camps, preschool, and fundraising
- **Do:** Speak to the unique complexity of JCC operations
- **Don't:** Assume JCCs operate like YMCAs — different community, different priorities
- **Don't:** Lead with features that feel dated or enterprise-heavy
- **Don't:** Ignore the fundraising integration story — it's a critical differentiator

### Amilia 9-Pillar Messaging Architecture (Approved Verbatim — Constance Miller, Nov 2025)

Use these pillars when writing JCC-facing competitive content. Each pillar carries an approved key message, strategic takeaway, and key benefits.

| # | Pillar | Headline |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Reporting & Dashboards | "From Excel Cleanup to Trusted Reports" |
| 2 | Marketing & Member Engagement | "Built In, Not Bolted On" |
| 3 | Modern, Secure Experience | "UX That Doesn't Break Down Backstage" |
| 4 | Partner Options, Flexibility & Choice | "A Platform That Plays Well with Others" |
| 5 | Payments & Financial Control | "One System, Fewer Surprises" |
| 6 | Roadmap, Support & Trust | "Innovation That's Real and Delivered" |
| 7 | Switching | "A Smarter Way to Transition" |
| 8 | Member Experience | "One Identity, One Journey" |
| 9 | Operational Cost Control | "One Platform, Your Choice" |

**Key messages by pillar (use as written or paraphrase tightly):**

1. **Reporting & Dashboards** — Staff shouldn't have to spend hours cleaning exports or justifying numbers before meetings. Dashboards and reports are configured to fit how your organization operates—so finance, leadership, and program staff can all work from the same page.
2. **Marketing & Engagement** — Sign-ups are just the beginning. Journeys should continue based on real actions that happen after open rates — like check-ins, lapses, and renewals. Daxko gives teams the tools to engage members without exporting lists or buying extra software.
3. **Modern, Secure Experience** — Members expect tools that are intuitive. Staff need workflows that make sense for real operations. With Daxko, both sides of the experience work securely, clearly, and without a learning curve.
4. **Partner Options** — Operations, reporting, and engagement can live in one platform—while tools like fundraising, ticketing, or facility access plug in as needed. You shouldn't have to choose between integration and control.
5. **Payments & Financial Control** — When payments, billing, and reporting live in the same system, finance teams can do their jobs without extra steps. Lower rates may sound appealing—but if the financials don't align, you pay for it somewhere else.
6. **Roadmap & Trust** — The function nonprofits need and ask for are already live and improving based on feedback from nonprofit leaders. Innovation should show up in the product, not just the pitch.
7. **Switching** — Change doesn't have to mean disruption. With structured onboarding, tested migration paths, and support built around your staff, you can switch systems without losing a season.
8. **Member Experience** — Members should be able to register, pay, and participate without managing multiple accounts. With one connected system, each family has a single digital identity and a smoother experience from start to finish.
9. **Operational Cost Control** — Whether you want everything in one system or need to keep the partners your team already relies on, a connected platform gives you options—and brings costs under control.

### Amilia AI Research Counter (Q1 2026 — Verbatim Approved Response)

**Context:** In Q1 2026, Amilia published research positioning themselves as a thought leader on "responsible AI in recreation," framing AI as a way to reduce digital friction for participants and administrative burden for staff — with a strong emphasis that AI should enhance (not replace) human connection.

**Objection:** *"Amilia is clearly leading on AI — they have research and a point of view."*

**Daxko Response (verbatim, approved by Constance Miller):**
> "They're doing good work to educate the market. The practical decision is how AI shows up in your day-to-day: reducing staff workload, improving participant clarity, and protecting trust. The research itself reinforces that privacy, accuracy, and preserving human connection are non-negotiable — so the best partner is the one that can operationalize AI responsibly inside core workflows without adding more complexity or governance burden."

**Discovery questions to redirect Amilia AI conversations:**
- "If AI is going to reduce friction, which 2-3 moments are your biggest pain points today: program search, registration, reminders, answering common questions, billing questions, or onboarding?"
- "Who will own AI governance — privacy, accuracy, escalation to humans, and ongoing tuning?"
- "When participants want instant answers, what's your standard for accuracy and how do you protect the human relationship?"
- "Do you want AI as an add-on initiative, or embedded in the core operational system your teams already rely on?"

**Operational Analysis ask (verbatim):**
> "Your peers are saying AI is necessary soon, but capacity is the constraint. Let's do a quick Operational Analysis to identify where automation, reporting, and engagement can reduce staff workload immediately — and where AI should (and shouldn't) be applied based on privacy and trust."
