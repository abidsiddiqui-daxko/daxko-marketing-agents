## Segment Intelligence

### Single-Location Health Club
- **Profile:** 500-5,000 members, for-profit, community gym or boutique health club
- **Primary buyer:** Club Owner / Operator — ROI and revenue growth focused
- **Secondary:** General Manager · Membership Sales Director · Finance/Billing Manager
- **Cycle:** 3-6 months. Owner is sole decision maker.
- **Pain:** Manual billing, high card declines, no automated lead follow-up, scattered tech stack
- **Triggers:** New location · Outgrowing software · High card declines · Staff admin burden · Launching digital membership sales

### Multi-Location Operator
- **Profile:** 2-50+ locations, 5,000-50,000+ members, franchise or corporate
- **Primary buyer:** VP of Operations / COO — standardizing across locations
- **Secondary:** CFO · IT Director · Membership Sales Director
- **Cycle:** 6-12 months. Committee-based. C-suite sign-off.
- **Pain:** Inconsistent member experience · No consolidated reporting · Complex corporate memberships · Migration risk
- **Triggers:** Acquisition · Franchise standardization · Scaling beyond current system · Board pressure on financial reporting

### Medical Fitness & Wellness
- **Profile:** Hospital/healthcare affiliated, mission-driven but for-profit or quasi-public
- **Primary buyer:** Director of Wellness
- **Pain:** Complex membership categories · Healthcare integration · Outcome reporting · Adaptive equipment scheduling
- **Proof point:** Lakeshore Foundation — 45-acre adaptive campus, 3 US Paralympic teams, 400+ athletes in 18 months, $1M/year x 3-year fundraising initiative

## Competitive Intelligence (Club)

| Competitor | Primary Weakness | Win Theme | Trap Question |
|-----------|-----------------|-----------|---------------|
| **ABC Fitness** | 7-10 day delay for basic changes; rigid payfac | Self-service speed of change; real-time reporting with accrual accounting; open API | "How much control do you actually have today over your billing, refunds, and when your funds hit your account?" |
| **Mindbody** | Studio/spa-first, lacks club depth; 20% marketplace fee | Purpose-built for clubs vs. broad wellness platform; multi-amenity and family membership depth | "How are you currently managing family memberships or non-class offerings like courts, childcare, or programs?" |
| **Jonas Fitness** | Frequent downtime, crashes, no native mobile, complex workflows | CA 99.9998% uptime; automated billing; native mobile; modern UX | — |
| **Zenoti** | Spa-first architecture, add-ons inflate cost | Purpose-built gym workflows vs. spa-adapted platform | "How do you currently manage family memberships or facility reservations like courts and swim lanes?" |

**Win conditions:**
- vs. ABC: when clubs want control over billing, refunds, fund timing; frustrated with delays
- vs. Mindbody: when multi-amenity needs, family memberships, complex operations
- vs. Jonas: when modern UX and uptime are decision factors
- vs. Zenoti: when multi-purpose facilities need gym-specific depth

**Lose conditions:**
- vs. ABC: large franchise already standardized on ABC's payfac model
- vs. Mindbody: boutique/studio-focused business prioritizing simplicity or marketplace exposure
- vs. Zenoti: when AI feature excitement or spa component is core to the business

## Market Challenges

1. **Revenue optimization** — Maximizing revenue per square foot in competitive market

## AI Positioning for Club

- AI embedded in operational systems they already trust — not a separate tool
- Approved Club AI use cases (CL-03):
  1. Predictive churn identification of at-risk members
  2. Automated re-engagement and renewals
  3. Campaign orchestration and content generation
  4. 24/7 conversational support — AI agents respond to prospect and member inquiries across voice, SMS, email, and web chat, capturing leads and resolving questions when staff aren't available
  5. Smarter communication — selecting the right message, timing, and channel based on member behavior
  6. Real-time member view — complete view of each member with summaries, engagement history, and next-best actions, helping staff prioritize outreach, personalize interactions, and manage the full member lifecycle (CL-03 Q9, Apr 2026)
- NOT applicable to Club: donor outreach / fundraising AI (that's Nonprofit-specific — the blank template incorrectly listed this; Kim corrected it)
- Club AI objection "We already use AI partners" → Full talk track: "That's great — many clubs are using third-party AI tools today for things like call handling or front desk inquiries. Daxko is designed to support that, and using a partner now won't limit your ability to adopt native capabilities later. Over time, Daxko Club Automation and Daxko Engage Pro will go deeper by embedding AI directly into operations — like booking, billing, and member actions — while still giving you flexibility and choice."
- Club AI objection "Will AI features from competitors actually improve operations?" → Clubs question whether competitor AI adds value or just complexity. Counter: Daxko AI is embedded in the tools they already use — no new system to manage, no data silos, no added complexity.
- Club-specific AI messaging rule: Always tie AI to measurable outcomes — lead conversion, payment recovery, member retention. Position as embedded within Club Automation and Engage Pro, not as a separate tool. Focus on how it improves daily operations for staff.
- Never: overhype AI as standalone; frame as separate from core operations

## Campaign Output Sections (required — from CL-05)

Every campaign brief includes:

1. **Market Context** — Current club market state, trends, competitive pressure
2. **Target Segment** — Specific club type, size, current tech stack, buyer persona
3. **Business Case Theme** — Single financial or operational outcome driving the campaign
4. **Value Propositions** — 2-3 specific, quantified benefits mapped to buyer pain

6. **Competitive Differentiation** — How CA/Motionsoft/Engage Pro wins against the named competitor

## Campaign Strategy Guardrails

### Always
1. Lead with financial impact and ROI — club operators are numbers-driven
2. Use specific benchmarks ("$150K reclaimed" — attributed as *revenue recovery through payment optimization*, platform-wide, not to any single mechanism). ⚠️ **"+5% card approval" is Daxko's 2026 TARGET (`okrs-and-priorities.md`: improve by 5% by Dec 31 2026), not an achieved result — never cite it as a customer outcome.** *(corrected 2026-09-22 — found by Agent 13 — Content Production's test suite)*
3. Address implementation concerns proactively — timeline, risk, migration plan
4. Highlight payment processing advantages — integrated, Flex Fees, Daxko Payment Services
5. Emphasize speed to value — how fast they go live and see results
6. Show cross-location value for multi-site operators
7. Speak operator language — ROI, efficiency, scalability, speed
8. Use "Club Automation" in full on first use — never abbreviate to "CA" in headlines

### Never
- Lead with technology features or specs
- Use mission-driven language (that's nonprofit territory)
- Ignore the competitive landscape — club buyers are comparison shoppers
- Use "small business" framing for enterprise-scale operators
- Promise timelines without checking with implementation team

## Anti-Patterns

**Mission-driven framing:**
> "Daxko partners with clubs to create healthier communities through technology..."
→ Wrong vertical. Club operators want revenue impact, not mission statements.
→ Better: *"[Customer name] recovered [$X]/month in failed payments within [N] days of switching to Daxko's payment processing."* 🔴 **ILLUSTRATION ONLY — the bracketed shape is deliberate. This is an example of how to PHRASE a claim, not a claim. There is no Metro Fitness customer; the earlier version of this line named one and read exactly like a real proof point, which is how an invented figure gets lifted into live copy.** *(corrected 2026-09-22 — found by Agent 13 — Content Production's test suite)*

**Feature dump:**
> "Campaign highlighting Daxko's member management, billing, scheduling, reporting, and mobile app features."
→ Too many things. Focus on one pain point, one outcome.
→ Better: "Campaign targeting multi-location operators losing visibility across sites — showing how centralized reporting reveals revenue opportunities they're currently missing."

**One-size-fits-all:**
> Same campaign for single-location owners and 50-site operators.
→ Different buyers, different pain, different budgets. Always segment.

**Analytics as a Service to single-location clubs:**
> Including Analytics as a Service positioning for standalone gyms.
→ Analytics as a Service is for multi-location operators. Confirm packaging before single-location positioning.
